Talking with Teens

Seven books. Six modules. One question worth asking your way through. A reader for the part of teaching, and the part of parenting, that nobody hands you a manual for.

7 Books 6 Modules ~13 Hours

Adolescence is the part of teaching nobody gives you a manual for. The training shows up for math instruction, for classroom management, for IEP paperwork. The actual experience of being in a room with twenty-five middle schoolers, half of whom are processing something they can't name, isn't on any syllabus. This is a reader for that part.

The structure is six modules. Each one pairs a short stack of readings with a verified author interview (podcast or video), walks you through the material using my district's Chunk-Chew-Check format, and asks for a small concrete artifact at the end. About two hours of work per module. Read them in order, or jump to the one your week needs.

Chunk-Chew-Check is the format my colleagues and I use with eighth graders, and it works on adult readers for the same reason it works on kids: it slows the input down enough that something actually gets metabolized. Chunk is the input (read this, watch this, listen to this). Chew is the processing move (what to look for, what to mark). Check is the artifact (a sentence to write, a script to draft, a paragraph for a substitute). The Check is the part that keeps this from being a podcast queue.

§ About

Where this came from

This is a reader I built for myself first, and then for the colleagues and parents who kept asking what I was reading. I teach 8th grade Social Studies at Alderwood Middle School in the Edmonds School District, and I've spent the last several years collecting the books, podcasts, and interviews that actually changed how I show up for thirteen-year-olds. This is the curated version.

The format borrows from my district's instructional design work. Chunk-Chew-Check is the same scaffolding I use with students; it turns out it works on adults reading dense developmental psychology for the same reasons it works on kids reading a primary source. The voice is teacher-to-teacher, and parent-to-parent. Not preachy, not corporate, not "edutainment." If a section feels like a PD slide deck, that's a bug, not a feature.

Every link in the library and every cited episode in the modules has been verified. If a link breaks, the speaker and episode title should be enough to find the new home through a quick search. If something feels off or you spot an error, please tell me. This is a working document, not a finished one.

Start with Module I if you want the foundation. Skip to Module II if you want sentences you can use tomorrow. Or open The Library if you'd rather browse the source material on your own.
